Macon County is WNC's proof that removal is winnable. On **July 14, 2026**, commissioners voted **5–0** to bar the Sheriff's Office from spending county funds on Flock and to terminate the contract "as soon as possible" — ahead of its October expiration. Motion by **John Shearl**, seconded by **Barry Breeden** (a former sheriff's deputy who came in "50-50"), with Chair **Josh Young** — who had leaned toward his sheriff until he polled constituents and found them "nearly unanimously against." Over 100 residents packed the room. Shearl's verdict on the Flock rep sent to defend the product: "like a car salesman." ([Smoky Mountain News](https://smokymountainnews.com/news/news-latest/de-flocking-macon-county-votes-to-let-alpr-contract-lapse/) · [the meeting video](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC59-D2PQ94Ax9NAfgyMgJKA/videos))

The numbers that did it: **16 cameras (12 county, 4 schools), a $31,950 contract grown to ~$47,000/year, logging 1–3 million plates a month in a county of 37,000.**

## The epilogue — what the record shows since

- **No reinstatement effort, and no crime-spike claims, as of mid-August.** Sheriff Holbrooks says he "respects the decision" while still defending the tool. The removal is holding.
- **The four school cameras are in limbo** — commissioners deferred them to school leaders; after the vote MCSO lost access to all 16 ("no one has access now"), and the school system says **neither MCSO nor Flock has contacted them** about the cameras on their buildings. No school-board vote has ever been taken. That's the unfinished 20%.
- **A cautionary note:** the fight's social-media spillover included **false claims that Franklin had Flock cameras** (it has none), escalating to threats against town officials — which handed officials a warning-about-the-opposition story. Accuracy is a tactical asset; see [[How Cities Won]] on what backfires.
- **The election carries it forward:** commission candidate **Dylan Castle** — Republican, pastor, county GOP vice-chair — spoke against Flock on constitutional grounds and advanced to the November general (an MCSO captain lost the same primary). Macon may elect WNC's first explicitly anti-ALPR commissioner.
