Just before midnight on May 12, 2026, after roughly six hours of waiting, Asheville City Council voted to accept $1.14 million in federal money and sign a 7.5-year contract with Axon Enterprises to build a Real-Time Intelligence Center — a police hub wiring together license plate readers, body cameras, drones, and private security cameras across the city. Nearly every one of the three dozen residents who spoke that night opposed it. Twenty-one more waited hours for their three minutes and were sent home unheard. The final contract was not available for anyone to read before the vote — five days earlier, the city manager had confirmed on the record that it **"does not exist yet."** Kim Roney cast the only no.

That's how mass surveillance came to Asheville: not through public debate, but around it. Cameras deployed two years before council was briefed. Money quietly requested from a congressman without council's knowledge. Funding buried on consent agendas. A vote pulled when too many people showed up, then rescheduled to the end of a budget meeting that ran past 10:30 PM.

**This site is the record.** Every vote, every quote, every dollar, every promise — sourced to primary documents, timestamped to the city's own videos, and dated. We are not neutral: we think a city that passes surveillance infrastructure this way has not earned the benefit of the doubt, and the record backs us up. But everything here is checkable, because a movement armed with verified facts is harder to dismiss than one armed with vibes.

## Why this exists

The RTIC is expected fully operational by **summer 2027**. The contract runs to **2033**. Oversight — the audits, the policies, the promises about ICE and facial recognition — happens *now*, in the years where attention usually fades. This is a living workbook: it updates as meetings happen, records surface, and promises get tested.

Found an error? Good — that's the point of publishing sources. Every page shows where its facts come from.
