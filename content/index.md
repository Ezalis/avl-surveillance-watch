Just before midnight on May 12, 2026, Asheville City Council voted 6–1 to accept $1.14 million in federal money and sign a 7.5-year contract with Axon to build a Real-Time Intelligence Center — a police hub wiring together license plate readers, body cameras, drones, and private security cameras. Nearly everyone who spoke that night opposed it. Twenty-one more residents waited hours and were sent home unheard. Five days earlier, the city manager had confirmed the contract council was authorizing **"does not exist yet."**

I live here. I started watching the meetings, pulling the transcripts, and filing the quotes — and this site is what that turned into: **the sourced record of how surveillance came to Asheville**, every vote, quote, and dollar tied to primary documents and timestamped to the city's own videos. I'm not neutral — I don't think a city that passes surveillance infrastructure this way has earned the benefit of the doubt — but everything here is checkable, because a movement armed with verified facts is harder to dismiss than one armed with vibes.

Here's the whole story in one look. Every entry links to a deeper page with the receipts.

## Why I built this

The RTIC is expected fully operational by **summer 2027**. The contract runs to **2033**. The oversight that matters — the audits, the policies, the promises about ICE and facial recognition, who gets hired to run it — is being decided *now*, in the years when attention usually fades. I wanted one place where a neighbor could understand the whole thing in ten minutes, check every fact behind it, and find a next step that fits them.

This is a living workbook: it updates as meetings happen, records surface, and promises get tested. Found an error? Good — that's the point of publishing sources. Every page shows where its facts come from.
