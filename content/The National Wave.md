---
title: The National Wave
description: 50–100+ communities have dropped Flock and similar systems. The reasons read like a warning label for Asheville.
---

Asheville signed up for this technology in the same year much of the country started walking away from it. Somewhere between 50 and 102 communities across 27+ states have canceled, rejected, or paused Flock contracts — roughly two dozen in 2026 alone.

## Why cities are leaving

- **Los Angeles** — the Inspector General found **1 in 3 stolen-vehicle alerts was a false positive**; every one led officers to stop or investigate an innocent driver. Contract dead, July 2026.
- **Austin** — a city audit found 10–20% of police searches had no documented reason; program ended June 2025.
- **Evanston, IL** — a state audit caught Flock giving CBP access to Illinois data in violation of state law. After termination, **Flock reinstalled the cameras without authorization**; the city issued a cease-and-desist and physically covered them.
- **Santa Cruz** — discovered **ICE had accessed city data ~4,000 times**. Canceled, January 2026.
- **Sedona, AZ** — officials said they were "misled and lied to" about data sharing.
- **Denver** — council rejected renewal unanimously; all ~110 cameras removed by March 2026.

## The abuse record

- A Texas sheriff searched **83,000 cameras nationwide** with the logged reason "had an abortion" — sweeping states where abortion is legal.
- **4,000+ lookups for immigration enforcement** by local police as informal favors to ICE, which has no Flock contract.
- Nearly **50 officers criminally charged** for tracking partners, exes, and family through these systems.
- Benn Jordan and security researcher Jon Gaines **rooted a Flock camera in under 30 seconds**; ~70 Flock cameras were later found streaming unauthenticated on the open internet. Congress has asked the FTC to investigate Flock's security.

## Does it even work?

Independent evidence is thin. The rigorous studies show plate readers help *solve* some cases but show **no deterrent effect on crime**. Flock's own "solves 10% of US crime" claim was publicly rejected by the academics whose institutions it invoked. Real-time crime centers show improved case clearance in some studies — and nothing resembling the transformation vendors sell.

## The legal horizon

The Fourth Amendment challenge to Norfolk's 175-camera Flock network is before the 4th Circuit — the appeals court whose rulings bind North Carolina. And in June 2026 the Supreme Court held that mass location data collection can be an unconstitutional search even when police only take a "small slice." The legal ground under these systems is moving.
