**Buncombe County Sheriff** (2018–present) · **seeking a 3rd term, Nov 2026** · Democrat

## Surveillance record

The architect of mass surveillance in Buncombe County. His office built the region's Fusus real-time intelligence center around 2022: **~1,800–2,000 cameras, including roughly 1,500 inside county schools**, plus 20 stationary and 2 mobile plate readers, with 59 people authorized to run Flock searches. Asheville paid $30K/year for access starting 2023; the city's RTIC is this network's expansion. ([Citizen Times via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/2-000-camera-network-feeding-090126108.html) · [Mountain Xpress](https://mountainx.com/news/local-government/wtf-buncombe-sheriffs-office-surveillance-technology-explained/))

He publicly credits the network for county crime reductions ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/crime-takes-a-dive-in-buncombe-county-sheriff-credits-community-connection-innovative-technology-north-carolina-state-bureau-of-investigations-annual-crime-report-real-time-intelligence-center)) — a causal claim the [[The National Wave|independent research]] does not support for these systems generally. His office touts a Fusus advisory board — "the only sheriff's office in the state" with one — though its membership, powers, and minutes are not public.

## The complicating fact

The record should hold this honestly: Miller has taken documented stands **limiting federal reach** — questioning ICE detainers on constitutional grounds and barring ICE from county schools without court orders. ([Bolts](https://boltsmag.org/buncombe-county-district-attorney-sheriff-elections-and-state-preemption/)) The tension is structural: the network he built is exactly the kind of infrastructure that outlives any one sheriff's restraint. His successor inherits the cameras, not his positions.

## The question for him

Will he publish the Fusus network's search audit logs and the advisory board's minutes? What binds his ICE commitments after 2030? Faces [[Gary Parris]] in November — see [[Election 2026]].
