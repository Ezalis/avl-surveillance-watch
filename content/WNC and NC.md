## Macon County took its cameras down

In July 2026, Macon County commissioners voted **5–0** to end their $47,000 Flock contract. Twelve cameras came down immediately. The sheriff's office had been logging **1–3 million plates a month in a county of 37,000 people**. The opposition was led substantially by Republicans — the county GOP organized against Flock, and a Republican commission candidate campaigned on removal. A resident at public comment: "I love my government, but I don't trust you as much as I trust my mother."

This matters for Asheville twice over: it proves removal is possible, and it proves this isn't a left-right issue — it's a surveilled-versus-surveillers issue.

## Around the region

- **Buncombe County** — the Sheriff's Fusus network: ~1,800–2,000 cameras (including ~1,500 in schools), 22 plate readers, 59 people authorized to search Flock. On the ballot in November — see [[Election 2026]].
- **Hillsborough, Pittsboro, Chatham County, Boone** — all canceled or paused Flock in the past year.
- **Henderson County** — ~39 cameras; a resident petition for removal has ~937 signatures.
- **Haywood County** — kept its 8 cameras in July 2026 after Macon's removal.
- **Madison County** — 15 cameras; opposition group "Madison for Privacy" active.

## The state is going the other way

The July 2026 NC budget made the SBI's highway plate-reader program permanent — the stated ambition is a camera at every entrance and exit to the state. NC law caps ALPR retention at 90 days and requires written policies, but unlike Illinois, California, or Washington, **NC law says nothing about sharing your data with federal agencies or other states**. That gap is exactly what Asheville's 500-mile sharing radius drives through.

## NC's first abuse cases arrived on schedule

July 29, 2026: a Charlotte officer became the first in NC criminally charged for an unauthorized Flock search. August 5: a Mooresville officer was arrested for stalking his ex-wife through Flock — 31 searches. Eleven more officers under investigation.
