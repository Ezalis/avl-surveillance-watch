The RTIC was approved by people on this November's ballot. Silence about it is a position too — and we log it.

**Key dates:** register by **Oct 9** · early voting **Oct 15–31** · Election Day **Tuesday, Nov 3**.

## Mayor — the only real referendum

- **Kim Roney** — the sole no vote on every surveillance expansion since 2023. On the record (May 28 debate): concerns about a "billionaire-owned, AI-fueled, for-profit tech company" working with ICE, surveilling without warrants.
- **Esther Manheimer** (incumbent) — voted for the RTIC; frames the $1.14M as cost savings over paying the county for access. Her campaign launch drew RTIC protesters.

## City Council — three seats, six candidates, near-total silence

Incumbents **Antanette Mosley, Sheneika Smith, and Maggie Ullman all voted yes** on May 12. None is campaigning on it. Challengers **Drew Ball, Keith Young, and Jeffrey Burroughs** have **no stated position** on the RTIC (Young's DSA questionnaire includes data-privacy language — a "local firewall" against feeding enforcement pipelines — but never names the RTIC, Fusus, or Flock).

Not one 2026 candidate questionnaire in Buncombe County — not DSA's, not BPR's, not the Chamber's — asked a single question about surveillance. **Ask them yourself, and send us the answer with a link or recording.**

## Sheriff — the Fusus race nobody's calling that

- **Quentin Miller** (incumbent) built the county's ~1,800-camera Fusus network and credits it for crime drops.
- **Gary Parris** (challenger) — no stated position found.

## Also on the ballot

- **DA:** Todd Williams — who wrote a letter supporting the RTIC — is retiring. **Martin Moore** won the decisive primary; no stated surveillance position.
- **NC-11:** Chuck Edwards, who earmarked the RTIC's $1.14M, withdrew Aug 5 after a House Ethics report. **Jennifer Balkcom (R)** vs. **Jamie Ager (D)** — neither has a surveillance record.
